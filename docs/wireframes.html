2. Home

Three top-level places, switched with L and R: Home, Library and Downloads. Home opens on what you were playing, already focused, so A resumes it.

The lower screen always describes whatever is focused above it: save status, time played, whether the game is on the device. Its buttons are for touch; the controller never needs them.

3. Library, one platform at a time

L and R move between platforms, like ES-DE's systems. Each platform's page will take its console's tone later; for now it's grey. Covers show whether a game is on the device and how far a download has got.

The lower screen holds the touch controls: filters, sort, and an A–Z jump. On the controller, Y searches and holding a direction scrolls faster.

Question: should "All platforms" be one huge grid at the start of the platform list, or not exist at all?

4. Game

One primary action, and it's always focused first: Play when the game is on the device, Download when it isn't, with the size. Everything else hangs off the menu on X, not off a row of buttons.

The lower screen shows the save history across devices, because saves are the one thing that must never be a surprise.

5. Choose the app a game opens in

From a game's menu. Any installed app can be chosen, not just known emulators. Below the list, you pick how far the choice reaches: this game, its collection, or the whole platform. This is the fix for Argosy's #651.

The lower screen spells out the rule chain, so it's never a mystery why a game opens where it does.
